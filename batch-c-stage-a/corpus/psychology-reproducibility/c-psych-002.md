# Investigating Variation in Replicability: A "Many Labs" Replication Project

## Source Snapshot

*Content sourced from Journal of Open Psychology Data (openpsychologydata.metajnl.com) and OSF project page (https://osf.io/wx7ck/).*

**Authors:** Richard A. Klein, Kate A. Ratliff, Michelangelo Vianello, Reginald B. Adams Jr., et al. (50+ authors)
**Published in:** Social Psychology (2014), Vol. 45, pp. 142-152; data paper in Journal of Open Psychology Data (2014), Vol. 2, Issue 1, e4
**DOI:** 10.5334/jopd.ad (data paper); 10.1177/1948550614547449 (main article)

### Study Design (verbatim from data paper)

"This dataset is from the Many Labs Replication Project in which 13 effects were replicated across 36 samples and over 6,000 participants. Data from the replications are included, along with demographic variables about the participants and contextual information about the environment in which the replication was conducted."

"Our sample was comprised of 6,344 participants recruited from 36 different sources including university subject pools, Amazon Mechanical Turk, Project Implicit, and other sources."

"The aggregate sample had a mean age of 25.98. Participant ethnicity was: 65.1% White, 6.7% Black or African American, 6.5% East Asian, 4.5% South Asian, 17.2% Other or Unknown. Participant gender was 63.6% female, 29.9% male, 6.5% no response."

### 13 Effects Tested (from the study materials listing)

The study materials consisted of 13 effects drawn from 12 papers, including: Sunk costs (Oppenheimer, Meyvis, & Davidenko, 2009); Gain versus loss framing (Tversky & Kahneman, 1981); Anchoring (Jacowitz & Kahneman, 1995); Retrospective gambler's fallacy (Oppenheimer & Monin, 2009); Low-vs.-high category scales (Schwarz, Hippler, Deutsch, & Strack, 1985); Norm of reciprocity (Hyman & Sheatsley, 1950); Allowed/Forbidden (Rugg, 1941); Quote Attribution (Lorge & Curtis, 1936); Flag Priming (Carter, Ferguson, & Hassin, 2011); and others.

### Key Result

10 of the 13 effects were successfully replicated across the 36 samples, demonstrating that many classic psychological findings are robust when tested with large, diverse samples and standardized protocols.

### Data Availability

"Data were collected in-lab and online through a standardized procedure administered via an online link. The dataset is stored on the Open Science Framework website." License: CC0 1.0 Universal.

## Research Notes

- This is a complementary replication project to c-psych-001, using a different methodology (many labs, many samples rather than one-to-one replication).
- The 10/13 (77%) replication rate contrasts with the 36-39% figure from OSC (2015).
- This high replication rate has been cited by Gilbert et al. as evidence that high-powered designs produce higher replication rates.
- The study is fully open-access with all data available on OSF.
- Covers different types of effects than OSC (2015), including classic social psychology paradigms.
