# 1,500 scientists lift the lid on reproducibility (Nature survey)

## Source Snapshot

*Content sourced from Nature news feature (https://www.nature.com/articles/533452a) and publicly accessible summaries (sciencesprings.wordpress.com, phys.org).*

**Author:** Monya Baker
**Published:** Nature, Vol. 533, pp. 452-454, 26 May 2016
**DOI:** 10.1038/533452a

### Key Survey Findings (verbatim from public summaries)

"More than 70% of researchers have tried and failed to reproduce another scientist's experiments, and more than half have failed to reproduce their own experiments."

"52% of those surveyed agree that there is a 'significant crisis' of reproducibility."

"73% of respondents think that at least half of the papers in their field can be trusted."

"Fewer than 31% think that failure to reproduce published results means the result is probably wrong."

### Survey Methodology

The survey was emailed to Nature readers and advertised on affiliated websites and social media outlets. It sampled 1,576 researchers across disciplines. The survey likely selected for respondents more aware of reproducibility concerns.

### Respondent Opinions on Solutions

"Nearly 90% — more than 1,000 people — ticked 'More robust experimental design', 'better statistics', and 'better mentorship'."

"About 80% of respondents thought that funders and publishers should do more to improve reproducibility."

"Even the lowest-ranked item — journal checklists — won a whopping 69% endorsement."

### Discipline Variation

Physicists and chemists expressed the greatest confidence in their respective fields, while medical professionals and biologists had the least confidence in reproducibility.

### Disconnect Between Belief and Action

"But only a minority of researchers — fewer than 20% — said they had ever been contacted by another researcher who was unable to reproduce their work."
