# Meta Platforms Ireland Limited v Data Protection Commission — Appeal and Industry Response

## Source Snapshot

*Content sourced from publicly available statements and news coverage (RegSol, jjckb.cn, enforcementtracker.com). Meta's full legal filing is not publicly accessible.*

### Meta's Official Response (verbatim from company statement)

Meta announced that it will be appealing the DPC's decision and will seek a stay with the courts to pause the implementation of applicable deadlines. Meta has consistently argued that its data transfers were lawful under the Standard Contractual Clauses framework.

### Industry Context

Meta is not the only company affected by the Schrems II ruling. The fundamental question of whether US surveillance law provides "essentially equivalent" protection to EU law affects all transatlantic data transfers, not just Meta's. The decision came amid ongoing negotiations for a new EU-US Data Privacy Framework (subsequently adopted as an adequacy decision in July 2023).

### Business Impact Analysis

According to enforcementtracker.com, this was the largest GDPR fine ever imposed. Prior major GDPR fines included: Amazon (€746 million, Luxembourg, 2021), WhatsApp (€225 million, Ireland, 2021), and Meta's separate €390 million fine (Ireland, January 2023) for GDPR violations related to personalized advertising legal basis.

### Other Meta GDPR Penalties (January 2023)

In January 2023, the Irish DPC fined Meta Platforms Ireland Limited €390 million total — €210 million for Facebook and €180 million for Instagram — for violations related to the legal basis for personalized advertising (Articles 5(1)(a), 6(1), 12, 13(1)(c) GDPR). Meta had relied on "contractual necessity" (Article 6(1)(b)) as the legal basis, but the EDPB determined this was not valid and that user consent was required.

"Meta assumed that the acceptance of the updated terms of use constituted a contract between Meta and the user, since the processing of the data would be necessary for the provision as well as the improvement of the services."

### Privacy Advocate Perspective (noyb/Schrems)

The complaints that triggered both the January 2023 advertising decision and the May 2023 data transfers decision were filed by privacy activist Max Schrems and his organization noyb (None Of Your Business). Schrems has been the driving force behind the legal challenges to EU-US data transfers since 2013.

### EU-US Data Privacy Framework (July 2023)

The European Commission adopted an adequacy decision for the EU-US Data Privacy Framework on 10 July 2023, potentially resolving the legal basis for transatlantic data transfers going forward — though privacy advocates have signaled they may challenge this framework as well.
